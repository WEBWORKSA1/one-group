The complete group travel planning guide

Budgets, group rates, deposits and rooming lists — without the chaos.

By One.Group Editorial · 10 min read

Organizing a trip for 10, 20 or 50 people is a project, not a holiday booking. The organizers who stay sane follow the same sequence: decide, budget, lock in, collect, communicate.

1. Decide the non-negotiables first

Before anyone suggests destinations, agree on: dates window, budget per person, trip purpose (relax, adventure, celebration, work) and group size range. A quick poll (try our poll tool) avoids weeks of chat debate.

2. Build a budget per person

Cost lineTypical share of tripTip
Flights / transport30–45%Group fares often hold seats at a fixed price with names due later
Accommodation25–40%Hotels commonly treat 10+ rooms as a group block
Activities & tours10–20%Private group tours can cost less per head than individual tickets
Food & drink10–20%Pre-book one group dinner; leave others free
Contingency5–10%Covers price changes and last-minute swaps

Use the expense splitter to show everyone their share up front.

3. Ask for group rates — properly

Hotels, airlines, coach operators and tour companies usually have dedicated group desks. Request quotes with the same brief for everyone so you can compare: dates, headcount, room mix, budget and flexibility. Or send one request through One.Group Quotes and let partners come to you.

Negotiation levers: flexible dates, off-peak days, a guaranteed minimum headcount, and asking for complimentary rooms or upgrades (some hotels offer one free room per set number of paid rooms).

4. Collect deposits early

Commitment only exists when money moves. Ask for a non-refundable deposit that covers your own deposit exposure, with clear dates for the balance. Put it in writing: what happens if someone drops out, and whether they can sell their spot.

5. Rooming list & documents

  • Full names exactly as on passports
  • Room pairings and bed preferences
  • Dietary and accessibility needs
  • Emergency contacts
  • Passport/visa validity checks

6. Protect the trip

Group travel insurance can cover cancellations, medical emergencies and delays for everyone under one policy. Compare options in group insurance quotes and read the policy wording — exclusions matter more than the headline price.

7. Communicate like a pro

Create one announcement channel (read-only) separate from the chat. Share a one-page itinerary, a packing list and a “who to call” card. Send reminders at 30 days, 7 days and 24 hours.

Common mistakes

  1. Booking before collecting deposits
  2. Letting the chat decide everything
  3. No contingency budget
  4. Ignoring cancellation terms
  5. Doing it alone — appoint a co-organizer

How to start a community from zero

A 30-day plan to your first 100 engaged members.

By One.Group Editorial · 9 min read

In this guide
  1. Pick a sharp niche
  2. Choose the right platform
  3. Your first 10 members
  4. Rituals that create habit
  5. From 10 to 100
  6. The 30-day checklist

Most communities don’t fail because of bad software. They fail because nobody knows exactly who the group is for, and nothing happens often enough for people to form a habit. Fix those two things and the rest becomes execution.

1. Pick a niche you can describe in one sentence

“A community for entrepreneurs” is a directory category, not a community. “Weekly accountability calls for bootstrapped SaaS founders under $10k MRR” is a community. A sharp niche does three jobs: it tells the right people “this is for you”, it tells everyone else “this isn’t”, and it makes conversation topics obvious.

Use this formula: [who] + [shared goal or identity] + [how you meet]. Examples: “New parents in Lisbon who want weekend walks”, “Hindi-speaking data analysts learning AI tools, live every Thursday”.

Test it: post your one-sentence pitch in two places where your people already are. If fewer than 5 people ask “how do I join?”, sharpen it before building anything.

2. Choose the platform your members already open daily

Rule of thumb: start where friction is lowest, not where features are richest. You can migrate once you have 200+ active members.

3. Recruit your first 10 by hand

Your first 10 members should be personally invited. Message people individually — not a mass post. Explain why you thought of them specifically. Ten people who know each other’s names beat 300 silent strangers.

  • Friends and colleagues who fit the niche
  • Active commenters in related forums, subreddits or LinkedIn posts
  • Local noticeboards, cafés, gyms and libraries (for in-person groups)
  • A free listing on One.Group

4. Create a ritual people can set their week around

Habits come from predictable rhythm. Pick one recurring ritual and never miss it for the first 90 days: a Monday goals thread, a Saturday 7am run, a monthly book night. Consistency matters more than production value.

Add a welcome ritual too: greet every new member by name within 24 hours and ask one easy question (“What are you working on this month?”). New members who post in week one are far more likely to stay.

5. Grow from 10 to 100

  1. Ask for introductions. After each great session: “Who else would love this? Invite them.”
  2. Publish outcomes. Share wins, photos and lessons publicly (with permission).
  3. Partner with adjacent groups. Co-host one event with a similar group per month.
  4. Give members roles. Hosts, greeters and moderators become your growth team.
  5. Run a small contest. “Best photo from this month’s meetup” generates shareable content.

12 ways to monetize a community

Turn the value you create into income that keeps your group running.

By One.Group Editorial · 11 min read

Money is not the enemy of community — burnout is. Most groups die because organizers can’t keep funding venue fees, software and their own time. Here are twelve models, ranked roughly from easiest to most involved. Run the numbers with our membership revenue calculator.

1. Paid membership

Monthly or annual access. Example: 1,500 members × 5% paying × $12/month ≈ $900/month before fees. Works best when there is clear, recurring value (live sessions, templates, accountability).

2. Tiered membership

Free tier for discovery, paid tier for depth. The free tier is your marketing; the paid tier funds operations.

3. Ticketed events

Charge per event with a margin above venue cost. 40 attendees × $15 margin = $600 per event.

4. Sponsorships

Brands pay to reach your audience: named sessions, newsletter slots, prizes. Package them — “Presenting sponsor for 3 months” sells better than one-off mentions.

5. Affiliate & referral partners

Recommend tools, travel, gear or services your members already buy, and earn a commission. Always disclose.

6. Courses & cohorts

Package your community’s expertise into a paid cohort. Higher price, time-boxed commitment.

7. Job board

Professional communities can charge employers to post roles to a targeted audience.

8. Group buying

Negotiate bulk discounts for members and take a small coordination fee — see bulk quotes.

9. Merch

Print-on-demand shirts, mugs and kits. Low margin, high identity value.

10. Donations & supporters

“Buy us a coffee” pages and supporter walls work for mission-driven and free groups.

11. Consulting & services

Organizers with credibility often sell done-for-you services to members or sponsors.

12. Content & ads

A blog, YouTube channel or podcast built from community knowledge can earn ad revenue and bring new members.

Pricing tips

  • Start higher than feels comfortable — you can always discount.
  • Grandfather founding members at a lower price.
  • Publish what the money funds. Transparency increases willingness to pay.
